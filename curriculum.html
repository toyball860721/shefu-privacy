Curriculum Overview

射覆：五行八卦课 · Shefu: Five Elements & Bagua Course

What this app is: a structured, offline introductory course on classical Chinese thought for complete beginners. It teaches the Five Elements, Bagua, the 64 hexagrams, and the historical "shefu" object-guessing exercise as a learning device. 21 chapters, 210 short lessons, 216 objects.

Learning loop: teach card → practice → immediate feedback → mistake notebook → spaced review → reference textbook. The method is inspired by micro-learning apps such as Duolingo, but the subject is traditional Chinese culture, not a foreign language.

What it is not: not a horoscope, astrology, palm-reading, fortune-telling or zodiac-report app. It does not ask for birth date/time, name, gender or location; it does not generate daily fortunes, compatibility reports, predictions or personalized advice; it has no "ask a question and get a reading" workflow.

Category: Education (primary) / Reference (secondary). Fully offline; no account, no server, no UGC, no ads, no analytics, no in-app purchases; one-time paid download.

Part A · Foundations (Chapters 1–11)

1Five Elements Street — sort everyday objects by what they do: water flows down, fire rises, wood grows outward, metal contracts inward, earth supports.
2Five Elements Practice — the generating and overcoming cycles, practiced as a game of "who checks whom".
3Yin-Yang Lane — solid and broken lines; reading a trigram from the bottom up.
4Bagua Building — the eight trigrams: shape, natural image, family position, temperament.
5Shefu Station — reading a hexagram sign and guessing the hidden object, as a classroom exercise in image-based reasoning.
6Lost and Found — matching objects to their trigram "owners".
7The Look of the Five Elements — color, shape, texture, taste.
8Classifying Things · Home — kitchen, living room, bedroom.
9Classifying Things · Street — people, vehicles, shops.
10Classifying Things · Nature — sky, animals, plants, food.
11The Image Tree — a visual map of the classical associations, from core images to traditional commentary.

Part B · Historical Methods (Chapters 12–21)

These chapters teach methods found in classical texts such as the Meihua Yishu. They are presented as fixed historical course material, with in-app disclaimers that they are for cultural learning only and must not be used for real-world decisions. The practice desk uses sample objects; it does not collect a personal question or produce a personal reading.

12Earlier Heaven Numbers — the number sequence assigned to the eight trigrams.
13The 64 Hexagrams — how two trigrams form a hexagram; names and structure.
14Number Casting — a historical method for deriving a hexagram from two numbers.
15Time Casting — a historical method using year, month, day and double-hour.
16Original / Mutual / Changed Hexagrams — how the three are derived and read as a sequence.
17Generating, Overcoming and Seasonal Strength — the classical vocabulary of ti (body) and yong (use).
18Object Analysis — deriving shape, material and function from a hexagram's images.
19Shefu Practice — applying the five-step method to sample objects; reflection on which image matched.
20One Hexagram, Many Readings — why different questions use different interpretive frames; common confusions.
21Image-Thinking — guided reasoning: demonstration → partially guided → independent.

Why this is not a duplicate of fortune-telling apps

Most I Ching apps on the App Store are divination utilities: enter a question, cast a hexagram, receive a reading. This app has none of that workflow. The only overlap is the classical subject matter—just as a course on Greek mythology overlaps with "mythology" apps but is not a duplicate of them. The product innovation is the learning method: 210 bite-sized lessons, active recall, error tracking, spaced review, a visual textbook, and game-like progression—without leaderboards, social features, or competitive mechanics.
